6. Chosen project and the user's problem

Tideway: an approval-gated cash-flow rescue agent for small businesses. First user: a small service business or wholesaler with regular invoices, supplier payments and payroll. The first version has one currency per business and one main workflow, and ships with three synthetic businesses in three countries (Bangladesh in BDT, China in CNY, Singapore in SGD). New businesses sign up as their own workspace, in any of 29 currencies.

Core question. "Can I pay salaries on Friday? If not, which bill should be pushed back, which customer should be asked to pay early, and what will happen if customers pay late?" The agent calculates the gap, compares feasible plans, negotiates with simulated counterparties and, after human approval, runs the approved action in the sandbox.
StepWhat the user seesWhat the system does
1 Import dataOverview and records; CSV importValidates transactions, invoices and bills completely before writing; quarantines suspicious text.
2 Understand riskCash weather, three scenarios, "N of 20 futures fall short"Exact daily cash projection with a 14-day look-ahead; customer payment profiles; seeded 1,000-run Monte Carlo.
3 PlanRecommended bundle and alternatives with cost, risk before and afterExact optimiser over deferrals, early-payment offers and reminders; mock financing compared, never executed.
4 ApproveReview and approve, PIN, typed amountMaker-checker; two people above the threshold; HMAC-signed, expiring mandate.
5 NegotiateAccept, counter-offer or reject per counterpartySimulated counterparties; a counter becomes a new proposal; a refusal triggers a re-plan.
6 ExecuteReceipt with before/after stateIdempotent, atomic execution; state hash and read-back verification.
7 ProveAudit chain, exports, tamper drillHash-chained audit with witness checkpoint; evidence JSON, CSV and draft ISO 20022 pain.001 (never sent).

Demo numbers (Shapla Services, synthetic BDT)

Opening cash 120,000; payroll 80,000; supplier bill 70,000; confirmed inflow 20,000. Scheduled minimum cash on 31 October = 120,000 + 20,000 − 80,000 − 70,000 = −10,000. With a minimum reserve of 20,000 the reserve shortfall is 30,000. The optimiser then finds the cheapest combination of deferrals and early-payment offers that restores the reserve on every day of the horizon and checks it against simulated customer lateness. Every plan shows its assumptions; a reminder is never counted as cash collected; a deferral only takes effect after the (simulated) supplier accepts.

Skills we will show separately

  • A verifiable action plan from an ambiguous financial request; plans that break a constraint are rejected.
  • Execution with permission; recovery after a failed call (same idempotency key returns the same receipt).
  • An honest "no safe plan" outcome with escalation.
  • Every number from the agent traced to a deterministic calculation and a source record.

Out of scope

Real-money transfers, production bank integration, automatic loan approval, stock trading, a full ERP, mixed currencies inside one business. The financing module is a clearly labelled mock catalogue comparison, not a promise of credit.

Data contract

Transactions: id, date, amount_minor, currency, type, category. Invoices: id, customer, amount_minor, due_date, expected_collection_date, status, paid_date. Bills: id, payee, payee_account, amount_minor, due_date, priority, reschedule_allowed. Policy: reserve_floor, action_limit, daily_cap, dual_approval_threshold, look-ahead days, autonomy level. Amounts are positive integers in minor units; the transaction type gives the direction.

Cash calculation and risk

Closing cash each day = previous day's cash + that day's inflow − that day's outflow, in three scenarios: scheduled, likely (each customer's expected delay) and stress (a high-percentile delay). Each open invoice's delay comes from TidePay's predicted distribution (likely = expected delay, stress = 80th percentile); the credibility blend of own history and the business-wide average (weight n/(n+5)) remains the explainable fallback. The Monte Carlo draws one delay per customer per simulated future and reports the probability of breaching the reserve, negative cash and unfunded payroll. We do not claim statistical forecast accuracy; the back-test is on synthetic history. Early-payment discounts are priced as an annual rate d/(1−d) × 365/days (2% for 20 days ≈ 37%). Financing comparisons disclose fee, term, repayment and eligibility assumptions.

Agent and tools

Tideway's own AI. TidePay predicts each open invoice's lateness band and is trained by simulated federated averaging across 40 synthetic SMEs in one process with illustrative, reproducible masks. The process holds the data and pools training features; no deployed privacy guarantee is claimed (log loss 1.036 against 1.55 for the old rule). TideLM is Qwen3-4B-Instruct with a LoRA adapter we trained with MLX on a laptop: it turns an owner's message in eight languages into a typed tool plan and turns pasted bank, card and mobile-money messages into draft records while flagging injection. On held-out examples with unseen names the deployed v2 adapter raised exact plans from 26.7% to 96.7% and extraction F1 from 0.834 to 0.969 (evidence/ml/tidelm_report.md). It runs on the owner's machine; plans pass a typed validator, replies are written by code from tool output, and the model has no money or write tool. Answers come in the language of the question (English, Chinese, Spanish, French, Portuguese, Indonesian), with the same numbers.

Agent tools: read_ledger, profile_customers, project_cash, simulate_risk, check_security, compare_plans. There is no approve or execute tool, no raw SQL, shell or arbitrary HTTP. Each request has at most eight tool calls. The default local agent needs no network. The optional LLM mode (own API key, per-message consent) sends only pseudonymised names and tool output, and flags any number in the answer that no tool produced.

Demo dataset: three synthetic businesses (Shapla Services in BDT, Lotus Print Studio in CNY, Harbourline Traders in SGD), each with settled history and open invoices and bills, designed so each tells a different story: a payroll gap, hidden risk from customer habits, and a case with no safe plan plus planted attacks. Synthetic and real results are never mixed.

8. Security and permission model

Security and compliance are 30% of the official rubric. The tiers and controls below are our implementation, not an organiser-published tier numbering, and not a production compliance certification.

TierWhoCan
ReadAgentRead records, project cash, simulate, analyse.
ProposeAgentCreate plan proposals and message drafts; no external mutation.
ApproveOwner or accountantApprove a bundle with PIN step-up; two people above the dual-approval threshold.
ExecuteOwner or accountantExecute a signed, accepted mandate once.
Delegated executeAgent under a standing mandateZero-cash reminders only, five a day.
DenyEveryoneReal money movement, credit decisions, payroll deferral, acting on unverified bank details. The model cannot override these.

Approval is more than a button

Approval requires a fresh PIN. The signed mandate binds the actions, amounts, counterparties, plan and snapshot hashes, policy version and a 15-minute expiry. If any field changes, the old approval is void. Before execution the system re-verifies the signature, expiry, the human or standing-mandate principal, that every counterparty accepted, and that the underlying records have not changed. The same idempotency key returns the same receipt; a second key on a used mandate is refused.

Controls

  • Kill switch and circuit breaker: pause stops planning and execution; only the owner resumes, with a reason. Five security denials in ten minutes pause the agent automatically.
  • Payee fraud: changed bank details and look-alike payee names block all actions on the bill. Only flagged payees can be verified: owner-only, by callback to a number on file. Whoever verified cannot be the only approver for that payee (separation of duties).
  • Injection: hidden Unicode is stripped and flagged; instruction-like text already in ledger records is quarantined as data and excluded from planning/model context. Inbox text is read locally only to draft records, then deterministically screened and held for human confirmation.
  • Autonomy dial: Advise, Propose or Delegate; moving money is never delegated.
  • Web layer: loopback only, Host and Origin checks, CSRF token, HttpOnly SameSite=Strict cookie, strict CSP, rate limits, spreadsheet-safe CSV export.

Privacy and credentials

No real passports or merchant identities in the repository. Minimum fields only; names are pseudonymised before any optional external model call and record free text is never sent. Credentials live in a local .env that is never committed.

Failure recovery and audit

Do not treat a timeout as failure and retry blindly: query by idempotency key first. Execution is atomic. A pending mandate can be cancelled; an executed action is not shown as "undone" (a reversal would be a separate transaction). The audit log is a hash chain with an independent witness co-signature, so edits and truncation are detected; a one-click drill demonstrates this on a safe copy. A hash chain alone does not protect against a fully privileged attacker.

Security report and test map

The security assessment contains assets and trust boundaries, the permission matrix, a threat table mapping each of the 30 self-test case ids to OWASP LLM and Agentic identifiers, known risks and residual limitations. It does not claim certification against any bank or jurisdiction.

Likely judge questions

  • "Why is this different from a general chatbot?" An exact cash engine and optimiser, backend policy enforcement, stateful execution; baseline on the same test set once it is run.
  • "What if the AI is wrong?" It cannot act. Figures come from tools; answers are grounding-checked; execution needs a signed mandate; refusal and clarification paths exist.
  • "Why is all the data synthetic?" Reproducibility and attack coverage; state it plainly; give any small consented pilot separately.
  • "Where is the novelty?" Not a generic forecast; the contribution is a constraint-aware plan through to safe action, with enforcement outside the model. We do not claim a novel research algorithm.
  • "No real bank integration?" The rules accept sandbox evidence for A to E; we show the adapter boundary (a draft ISO 20022 file that is never sent). No production-readiness claim. [S2]
  • "Who would pay?" Small business owners or accountants; a subscription hypothesis; say that pricing is not validated.
  • "Is the mandate the AP2 standard?" It is modelled on the AP2 idea (verifiable, scoped, expiring authorisation); it is not an implementation of the specification.

Demo backup: fixed seed fixtures, a reset path, an offline recorded video, PDF screenshots, and fallback if a model times out. Never present a recording as live execution.

Model API BDT 3,000 to 7,000 (only if the optional LLM mode is evaluated); hosting and storage 0 to 2,000; interview, mobile data and incidentals 1,000 to 2,000; contingency 1,000 to 2,000. Total roughly BDT 5,000 to 13,000, excluding existing laptops and team time. Do not assume free credits; set a daily usage cap. Run 10 workflows first and measure cost per run; budget = expected runs × measured cost per run + hosting + buffer. Paid tools are not required for video editing or slides. The exchange rate and net amount of any prize are not estimated here.

Clarify before travel

How many tickets and accommodations; direct booking or reimbursement; airfare cap; airport transfer; visa fee; meals; insurance; arrival and departure dates. No non-refundable booking at our own cost without written confirmation. An invitation letter does not guarantee a visa.

Value after the competition

Win or not, the reusable outcomes are working code, a security test suite, audit design, user interviews and a short product demo. Before any real deployment, a real bank contract, jurisdiction-specific legal review, stronger security review and data-processing agreements would be needed; these are not deliverables of this demo.

Business-model test: show the workflow to 5 SME owners and 2 accountants; ask what they would pay for, how they do it today, and what stops them uploading data. Do not manufacture market size or revenue from interviews.
