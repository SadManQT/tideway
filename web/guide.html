01 · Orientation

What Tideway does

A small business often knows its balance today but not the shape of the next two weeks. Tideway turns the records already in the workspace into a dated, source-linked decision: what may happen, why it may happen, and what a human could choose to do about it.

01

Find the gap

Exact minor-unit arithmetic walks a daily ledger of opening cash, expected invoice inflows, supplier bills and payroll. Scheduled, likely and stress views make the assumptions visible.

Output: a minimum-cash date, reserve shortfall, warnings and a cash-weather label.

02

Explain the risk

TidePay gives open invoices a probability across five lateness bands. A seeded Monte Carlo run turns those probabilities into a repeatable estimate of reserve, negative-cash and payroll risk.

Output: a risk read-out a judge can reproduce from the same records and date.

03

Propose a rescue

An exact search compares supplier deferrals, early-payment offers and zero-cash reminders. It keeps payroll funded, respects the reserve floor, excludes blocked payees and explains rejected candidates.

Output: up to three bundles, or an honest “no safe plan” escalation.

04

Keep people in charge

A proposal is not an action. PIN step-up, role checks, dual approval above the threshold, a signed expiring mandate, simulated counterparty agreement and a one-time read-back gate the sandbox execution.

Output: a receipt and audit evidence, only after the required humans act.

02 · The case for it

The problems it fixes — and how

Each answer is deliberately bounded. Tideway does not promise to remove uncertainty; it makes the uncertainty, trade-offs and stopping points easier to inspect.

A

“The account is fine today, but payroll is next week.”

Static balances hide dated inflows and outflows, and a deferral can simply move a shortfall past the chosen date.

How Tideway responds

Daily projection uses three scenarios and a target-date look-ahead (14 days by default, configurable within policy). A plan must hold the reserve floor on every day of that horizon.

B

“The biggest invoice is not necessarily the biggest risk.”

A customer’s payment habit, amount and timing matter together. A single late payer can distort a payroll decision.

How Tideway responds

Customer profiles blend the customer’s settled history with the portfolio, then TidePay and the counterparty risk map show both lateness likelihood and expected cash impact.

C

“A plausible plan is still not permission to act.”

An assistant that can both suggest and send creates an authority problem, especially when a counterparty or model is wrong.

How Tideway responds

The local agent has read/propose tools only. Humans approve with PINs; high exposure needs two different people including the owner; a signed, scoped mandate expires and is checked at execution.

D

“A message can look like a record and act like an instruction.”

Bank alerts, chats and ledger free text can contain hidden characters, changed account details or prompt-injection language.

How Tideway responds

Deterministic screening flags injection and payment-detail changes; suspicious stored text is quarantined. Inbox extraction creates drafts only, and nothing is saved until a person confirms it.

E

“When something happens, the team needs to show why.”

Approvals, counter-offers, retries and refusals are hard to reconstruct from an ordinary activity feed.

How Tideway responds

Per-business audit entries form a hash chain with an independent witness checkpoint. Evidence view verifies the chain, and exports include evidence JSON, records/receipts CSV and a draft pain.001 file.

03 · Under the hood

One request, one explainable response

The UI action is simple. The path underneath it is a sequence of checks, deterministic services and human gates. Here is the concrete Shapla Services payroll-gap story from the demo.

01

Ask

Nadia selects Shapla Services, sets 31 Oct 2026 as the target date, and chooses Run the rescue agent or asks the Assistant for a briefing.

02

Guard the request

The loopback HTTP layer checks the session, business membership, role, Host/Origin, CSRF token, body size and endpoint fields before the service sees a write.

03

Build the facts

The service walks exact integer minor units, maps invoice profiles, projects scheduled/likely/stress dates, and runs the seeded 1,000-future risk calculation.

04

Compare choices

The planner searches bounded combinations of allowed supplier deferrals, early-payment offers and reminders. It keeps payroll funded and the reserve floor intact through the look-ahead.

05

Return evidence

The browser receives a cash-weather result, source records, risks, rejected reasons and up to three proposal bundles. A response is information and a proposal—not an approval.

06

Gate authority

If Nadia reviews a bundle, the store checks PIN and role. Above the dual-approval threshold, Imran must approve separately and type the exact exposure too.

07

Act once, then read back

After simulated counterparties accept, the signed mandate, expiry, state hash, pause state and idempotency key are checked. The sandbox transaction commits once, returns before/after hashes, and appends evidence.

Concrete example · Shapla Services

What the judge should see

Opening cash is BDT 120,000; payroll is BDT 80,000; a supplier bill is BDT 70,000; a confirmed receivable is BDT 20,000; the reserve floor is BDT 20,000. The scheduled minimum is BDT −10,000 on 31 Oct 2026, a reserve shortfall of BDT 30,000.

The response can propose a bounded bundle. It cannot claim the supplier agreed before negotiation, move a real balance, or turn a mock financing catalogue item into an executable offer.

04 · Field guide

Every major feature, with the exact path to use it

Open a card for the operator steps, expected result and the boundary a judge should ask about.

01 · Overview

Cash weather & scenarios

Scheduled, likely and stress projections show when cash crosses the reserve floor, including the default 14-day look-ahead.

How to use it
  1. Sign in from the landing page and select a demo business.
  2. Open Cash weather; choose a target date in the top bar.
  3. Read the minimum cash, scenario cards, warning reasons and seeded risk fan.
  4. Use Read my brief for a spoken/read-aloud summary.

Boundary: the projection is a scheduled estimate from synthetic records, not a bank balance or guarantee.

02 · Forecasting

TidePay customer profiles

TidePay predicts five lateness bands per open invoice; explainable customer profiles remain the fallback when history is thin.

How to use it
  1. From Overview, open the customer or invoice explanation beside the forecast.
  2. Compare on-time rate, expected delay, p80/p90 delay, grade and confidence.
  3. Ask the Assistant which customer is the biggest risk, then open its tool plan.
  4. Use the back-test shown in the UI as synthetic evidence, not a production accuracy claim.

Boundary: TidePay was trained and evaluated on synthetic data; it does not make a binding credit decision.

03 · Risk

Seeded Monte Carlo

One thousand repeatable futures estimate reserve breaches, negative cash, unfunded payroll, expected/p90 shortfall and a common breach date.

How to use it
  1. Set the same business and target date to reproduce a result.
  2. Read the weather label: Clear, Unsettled or Storm, and open the scenario detail.
  3. Compare risk before and after a proposed bundle in Rescue.
  4. Point judges to the seed rule: business, snapshot hash and target date.

Boundary: only payment timing is uncertain in this model; it is not a general market forecast.

04 · Prioritisation

Counterparty risk map

Each customer is stressed alone—30 days late and then never paying—to rank expected cash impact beside supplier deferral options and fraud blocks.

How to use it
  1. Open Ask Tideway and ask, “Who is my biggest risk?”
  2. Open the answer’s details to see which read tools were chosen.
  3. Compare the customer’s cash lost by the horizon and extra days under reserve.
  4. Use the result to decide which record to inspect, not to label a customer as bad.

Boundary: this is a scenario ranking over the workspace records, not a credit report.

05 · Rescue

Exact planner & What-if

The planner searches bounded combinations and ranks feasible bundles; What-if lets an operator move a payment date or add an expense without saving it.

How to use it
  1. Open Rescue plan and press Run the rescue agent.
  2. Read the recommended and alternative bundles, source records, exposure and risk.
  3. Open What-if to adjust a customer delay or add a dated expense.
  4. Press reset or leave the view; What-if changes remain a simulation and are never written to the ledger.

Boundary: payroll cannot be deferred; blocked payees and over-cap bundles are rejected.

06 · Counterparties

Negotiation & mock financing

Rules-based simulated suppliers and customers accept, counter or reject. Financing is a labelled comparison of three mock products, never an executable offer.

How to use it
  1. Run a Rescue plan and review the requested actions.
  2. Choose Send requests only after understanding the proposed terms.
  3. Read each simulated reply; a counter becomes a revised proposal needing fresh approval.
  4. Open financing comparisons for fee, term and time-to-cash context only.

Boundary: simulated counterparties are fixed rules, not people; nothing contacts a supplier, lender or customer.

07 · Authority

PIN approval & signed mandates

Approvals are maker-checker controls. A second person and typed exposure are required above the configured threshold; the final mandate is scoped and short-lived.

How to use it
  1. Review the exact bundle in Rescue and choose Review and approve.
  2. Enter the operator PIN; type the exact exposure when prompted.
  3. Switch to the second eligible demo operator if dual approval is required.
  4. Inspect the mandate’s actions, amounts, snapshot/policy binding and expiry before negotiating or executing.

Boundary: viewers cannot approve, and a mandate never authorises real money movement.

08 · Completion

Execute once & read back

Execution checks accepted simulated replies, signature, expiry, pause state and fresh record/state hashes, then returns a receipt with before/after hashes.

How to use it
  1. After the required approvals, choose Send requests and wait for replies.
  2. Choose Execute once; do not treat a proposal or counter as execution.
  3. Read the receipt and its idempotency outcome.
  4. Retrying with the same key returns the same receipt; a new key on a consumed mandate is refused.

Boundary: execution mutates only the local sandbox and cannot move a bank balance.

09 · Conversation

Assistant & TideLM

Ask in English, 中文, Español, Français, Português, Bahasa Indonesia, Hinglish or Banglish. The answer uses tool output and shows the typed plan underneath.

How to use it
  1. Open Ask Tideway and start with “hi” for a live personal briefing.
  2. Ask a concrete question such as Nila Traders 晚20天付款会怎样？.
  3. Expand How Tideway worked this out to inspect the tool plan.
  4. Use a suggested follow-up; try “pay Rahman now” to see that the assistant has no payment tool.

Boundary: the model may read/propose; validators allow-list tools, and replies are composed from tool output rather than model authority.

10 · Capture

Zero-entry Inbox

Paste a bank, card, mobile-money alert or chat message; the local extractor drafts a record and the deterministic scan flags injection or payment-detail changes.

How to use it
  1. Open Inbox and paste the synthetic message from the demo script.
  2. Press Extract; read the red warning and flagged text.
  3. Check the proposed date, amount, counterparty and record type.
  4. Save only after confirming the clean fields; suspicious text remains data and is not an instruction.

Boundary: drafts are not records until confirmed, and the extractor cannot approve or execute.

11 · Ledger

Records, CSV & quarantine

Transactions, invoices and bills are validated before storage. CSV import is all-or-nothing; suspicious free text is quarantined and spreadsheet formulas are neutralised on export.

How to use it
  1. Open Records and choose Import CSV or download the example.
  2. Use the required record_type column and keep one business currency.
  3. Review validation and quarantine results before writing.
  4. Filter records by type/status and open quarantined text as data, not instructions.

Boundary: a bad row rejects the whole import; the limit is 5,000 rows or 1.5 MB.

12 · Security centre

Fraud blocks & injection defence

Changed bank details, look-alike payees, hidden Unicode and instruction-like text are surfaced or blocked before an action can proceed.

How to use it
  1. Open Security centre and run the self-test to replay the 30 defined cases.
  2. Open a flagged payee and use the owner-only callback verification path when the synthetic fixture requires it.
  3. Inspect separation of duties: the verifier cannot be the only approver for that payee.
  4. Open blocked records and read the structured reason instead of bypassing it.

Boundary: 30/30 is evidence for listed synthetic cases only, not proof that every attack is stopped or a certification.

13 · Control

Autonomy dial & kill switch

Advise reads and explains; Propose creates plans; Delegate allows only capped zero-cash reminders under a standing mandate. Pause and circuit-breaker controls stop the agent.

How to use it
  1. Open Security centre and read the permission matrix.
  2. Change the autonomy setting only as the owner, with the required PIN.
  3. Use the kill switch with a written reason to pause planning/execution; only the owner resumes.
  4. Review the denial counter after five security denials in ten minutes.

Boundary: moving money and payroll deferral are never delegated; the agent principal cannot execute a money mandate.

14 · Workspaces

Businesses, roles & team invites

Sign-up creates a business and owner; invite codes add an owner, accountant or viewer within plan seat limits. Every request is scoped to a member’s business.

How to use it
  1. From the landing page, choose Start free, enter business name, currency, cash, reserve, username and a valid 6–12 digit PIN.
  2. Open Team as owner and create a one-time invite code.
  3. Join with that code using a role and separate PIN; switch businesses only from the picker.
  4. Test the boundary: a viewer can read but cannot approve.

Boundary: plans and prices are illustrative; nothing is charged, and tenants cannot see one another’s data.

15 · Proof

Evidence, audit & exports

The Evidence view verifies the hash chain and witness checkpoint, records the first invalid entry, and offers a tamper drill plus local exports.

How to use it
  1. Open Evidence after a proposal, approval or execution.
  2. Run verification and read the chain status and witness result.
  3. Run the tamper drill to see an edited copy detected.
  4. Export evidence JSON, records/receipts CSV or the draft ISO 20022 pain.001.001.09 file.

Boundary: the payment file is a draft that is never transmitted; a local witness reduces but cannot eliminate privileged-host risk.

16 · Reach

Currency and language coverage

Workspaces choose one of 29 ISO 4217 currencies; the demo shows BDT, CNY and SGD. Screening covers multiple languages and common bank/wallet identifiers.

How to use it
  1. Choose a supported currency during sign-up; keep every record in that business currency.
  2. Ask the Assistant in one of its supported languages and compare the same numbers.
  3. Paste a synthetic alert in Inbox to see multilingual draft extraction and screening.
  4. Use display conversion only as illustrative viewing; calculations and mandates remain native.

Boundary: mixed currencies inside one business are rejected, and display FX is not live exchange data.

17 · Optional

Optional cloud-LLM mode

The default local agent and TideLM run on-device. An opt-in OpenAI-compatible mode can use a user key and per-message consent with pseudonymised names.

How to use it
  1. Copy .env.example to .env only on your own machine and set your own key; never commit it.
  2. Restart the app and opt in for a specific Assistant message.
  3. Read the consent text before sending; only pseudonymised names and tool output are eligible.
  4. Inspect grounding flags when model text contains a number not present in tool output.

Boundary: optional live-provider behaviour has not been measured in this repository; no live-LLM result is claimed.

05 · Reproduce the story

Setup, demo credentials and the honest scope

A judge should be able to start from a clean checkout, see the same synthetic story, and know exactly what a credential or result means.

01 · Requirements

Run the default offline prototype

  • Python 3.11.11 (the supported version is pinned in .python-version).
  • Python standard library for the default runtime; no paid service or package install is required.
  • A modern browser. The server binds to loopback only.
  • Node 22+ and Google Chrome are needed only for the screenshot/PDF helper scripts.
python3 run.py
# open http://127.0.0.1:8787

For a reproducible check, use make test. For a clean isolated sandbox, build the supplied Dockerfile and map 127.0.0.1:8787.

02 · Optional model path

TideLM stays optional

The app works without the adapter: the deterministic local agent answers instead. On Apple Silicon, the repository documents downloading the deployed adapter, installing mlx-lm, and evaluating it locally. This path is not needed for the core demo.

python3 scripts/get_tidelm.pypip install mlx-lm

The optional model is trained on synthetic examples. Do not describe it as a live-provider result or as a guarantee.

04 · Two-minute judge route

Show the complete loop

  1. Run python3 run.py; sign in as Nadia (Owner).
  2. On Shapla Services, open Cash weather and press Read my brief.
  3. Ask Tideway in English or another supported language; expand its tool plan.
  4. Run Rescue, inspect the exact bundle, then approve with Nadia’s PIN and typed exposure.
  5. Switch to Imran for the second approval, send simulated requests, and execute once.
  6. Use Inbox with the injection sample, then Security centre self-test and Evidence tamper drill.
!

Read the sandbox label before reading any number

All businesses, customers, suppliers, messages, balances and credentials in this project are synthetic. Financing products are labelled MOCK. The ISO 20022 payment file is a draft and is never sent. There is no production bank integration, lender decision, payroll deferral, payment rail, or claim that cash improves by a particular percentage. The self-test is evidence for its listed cases, not a certification.

Contest evidence update

A changed ledger needs fresh authority

On a fresh Shapla demo, approve the original rescue, then use the separate owner/PIN operation under What-if to move Bay Logistics to 15 November. Read-only scenarios still save nothing. The explicit ledger update invalidates old approvals and mandates; the fresh plan, counter-offer and execution go through the ordinary human gates.

Prediction audit

A versioned chronological benchmark preserves the historical report, fixes leaked prior statistics, and saves same-record predictions, split hashes and reliability bins. It is separate from shipped model weights.

Source: evidence/upgrade-v1/tidepay/report.json and docs/TIDEPAY_EVALUATION_V2.md

Distinct conditions

Development and held-out ledgers separate successful rescues, no action, infeasibility and blocked inputs. Screening reports include benign flags and missed attacks; scenario handling is not a claim that every case has a safe rescue.

Source: docs/SCENARIO_EVALUATION_V2.md

Visible model tools

The trace distinguishes deterministic preflight from model-selected calls after tool results. A missing model has a labelled fallback. Injected outputs test guards; current real-device inference remains outstanding.

Source: docs/MODEL_REASONING_V2.md

Repeatable live demo

Use a new database filename rather than erasing old evidence. Apply the explicit delay, demonstrate stale execution refusal, obtain fresh approvals and simulated consent, then verify read-back.

Source: docs/PAYMENT_DELAY_RUNBOOK.md. Video recording remains deferred.
